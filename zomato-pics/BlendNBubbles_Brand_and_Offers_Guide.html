02 — Type

Typography

Two free Google Fonts. Poppins for anything that should grab the eye, Open Sans for anything someone has to read.

Headline font
Poppins

Rounded, friendly, modern. Use for headlines, prices, offer flags and big numbers. Weights: 600 / 700 / 800. Avoid long paragraphs in Poppins.

Body font
Open Sans

Clean and highly legible at small sizes. Use for descriptions, body copy, terms and captions. Weights: 400 (regular) / 600 (semibold) / 700 (bold). This paragraph is Open Sans 400.

DoSet prices and offer numbers in Poppins bold. Keep body text in Open Sans. Use sentence case for headlines, it feels friendlier than ALL CAPS.
Don'tDon't run paragraphs in Poppins, don't mix in a third font, and don't shrink terms below 12px. No drop shadows on text.

03 — Logo

Using the mark

The roundel is the hero. Protect it: give it room, keep it teal, and never let an AI tool redraw the wordmark.

Do • Keep clear space around the badge equal to the height of the "B" in the wordmark.
• Minimum size 40px tall on screen / 15mm in print so the line art stays readable.
• Place on cream, deep teal, or a darkened photo. Use the cream badge version on busy or dark backgrounds.
• The wordmark reads FRUIT TEA & BUBBLE TEA. Use the real logo file every time.
Don't • Don't let Midjourney / AI generate or "fix" the logo, it garbles the text (you already caught "PRUIT TEA").
• Don't stretch, rotate, recolour or add effects.
• Don't place the teal logo on a low-contrast or photo background without the cream badge behind it.
• Don't rebuild it in a different font.
Asset to add: drop your master logo files (vector SVG/PNG, transparent) into the brand folder and link them here so this guide carries the real mark, not the decorative placeholder on the cover.
04 — Imagery

Photography & imagery

For a neighbourhood shop, real beats glossy-fake. People trust "that's the actual drink I'll get". Shoot the product; use graphics around it, not instead of it.

Do • Shoot your actual cups in natural daylight, drink as the hero.
• Show the boba and the layers, condensation on the cup reads as fresh.
• Consistent props and a clean, on-brand backdrop (cream, teal, wood).
• Add the football / seasonal energy with graphics over a real photo.
Don't • Don't rely on AI splash renders, the physics give it away (sealed lid but liquid flying, pasted-on lighting).
• Don't mix wildly different lighting across a grid.
• Don't bury the product behind effects.
• Don't post anything where the logo text is unreadable.

07 — Reusable

How to write any offer

So future offers sound like the same brand. Three parts, every time: the benefit, the condition, the nudge.

1 · BENEFITLead with what they get

Plain and concrete. "30% off", "Free topping", "Combo ₹329". Never bury it.

2 · CONDITIONState the catch once

"on your first order", "over ₹300", "dine-in only". One short clause, no jargon.

3 · NUDGEA warm reason to act

"made fresh, now an easy yes", "bring the squad". Friendly, never pushy.

Voice check: would you say it to a regular across the counter? If it sounds like a bank, rewrite it.
08 — Voice

Tone of voice

Young, warm, proudly Taiwanese-inspired. Friendly mate behind the counter, not a corporate flyer.

Sounds like us
  • "Real boba, made fresh."
  • "First sip's on us."
  • "Bring the squad."
  • "Brown sugar, the way it should be."
Doesn't sound like us
  • "Avail our exciting offer now!"
  • "Best-in-class beverages."
  • "Hurry, limited period only!!!"
  • Three exclamation marks. Ever.
